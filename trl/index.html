Frontline hiring isn't corporate hiring with more applicants. It runs on different rules, and the employers still applying corporate assumptions to it are the ones losing shifts.

On the table

Four questions, and nobody gets to dodge.

These are the actual questions going to the panel. No pre-approved answers, no vendor demos.

01

Is speed the quality strategy for hourly hiring? And where does that stop being true?

In corporate hiring, moving fast can be a red flag. On the frontline it might be the whole game. So what happens with a licensed nurse, a CDL driver, a shift manager? Tell us where "fastest wins" breaks down.

02

Drowning in applicants, still can't staff Saturday. Is that a top-of-funnel problem?

Or are employers buying more applicants to cover for what happens after people apply? This is the one we expect to disagree on, which is exactly why it's on the list.

03

The interview is the last human moment. Should it stay that way?

Screening, scheduling, and offers can mostly be automated now. Where does each panelist draw the line, and what do candidates deserve to be told about the AI they're talking to?

04

"We need an AI strategy." For frontline hiring, is that the wrong thing to ask for?

Boards want transformation. Frontline leaders need permission to fix two things really well. What should a CHRO actually bring upstairs?
